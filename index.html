Overview

This project examines NFL offensive performance from the 2020 through 2025 seasons to better understand what makes an offense successful. The analysis looks at passing and rushing tendencies, EPA per play, third-down performance, field position, score differential, explosive plays, and performance by quarter. The results show that offensive success is connected to several factors rather than one single statistic.

Data & Methodology

The data comes from NFLverse play-by-play files covering the 2020–2025 seasons. The six raw files contained 294,989 observations. The analytical sample was cleaned to standard offensive passing and rushing plays with an identified offensive team, leaving 210,719 plays.

EPA (Expected Points Added) is the primary efficiency measure. Positive EPA means a play improved the offense's expected scoring position; negative EPA means it worsened it. Success rate and yards per play are supporting measures. An explosive play is defined here as a pass gaining at least 20 yards or a run gaining at least 10 yards. Home/away status, turnover, score state, and explosive-play indicators were derived during cleaning.

Eight Findings

Finding 1

NFL Offenses Have Shifted Slightly Toward the Run Since 2020

Passes accounted for 58.95% of offensive plays in 2020 compared with 56.99% in 2025, a decrease of 1.96 percentage points. Rushing rose from 41.05% to 43.01%. Passing remained more common, but the overall mix became slightly more run-heavy.

Finding 2

Buffalo Led the NFL in Offensive Efficiency

Buffalo averaged 0.137 EPA per play, compared with the league average of 0.003. Kansas City ranked second at 0.109, Green Bay third at 0.102, and San Francisco fourth at 0.075. Buffalo also posted a 48.80% success rate and 6.03 yards per play.

Finding 3

Passing Remained More Efficient Than Rushing

Passing generated 0.017 EPA per play versus -0.015 for rushing and averaged 6.18 yards compared with 4.52. Passing also had higher success, first-down, and touchdown rates, although its 2.87% turnover rate was higher than the 0.64% rate on runs.

Finding 4

The Most Efficient Offenses Also Excelled on Third Down

Buffalo led with a 48.59% third-down conversion rate, followed by Kansas City (47.49%), Green Bay (45.83%), and San Francisco (45.05%), versus a 40.94% league average. These were also the top four offenses in overall EPA per play, showing a strong association between sustaining drives and overall efficiency.

Finding 5

Better Field Position Generally Improves Efficiency, but the Relationship Isn't Linear

Teams backed up inside their own 20 averaged -0.023 EPA/play. In the opponent's 21–50, that rose to 0.019, and inside the opponent's 10 it reached 0.039. The opponent's 11–20 was an exception at -0.005, so the relationship is meaningful but not perfectly linear.

Finding 6

Teams Pass More When Trailing and Run More When Leading

Teams trailing by 15+ points passed on 74.54% of plays. Teams leading by 14+ passed on only 45.87% and ran on 54.13%. That is a 28.67 percentage-point swing in pass rate between large deficits and large leads.

Finding 7

Baltimore Led the NFL in Explosive-Play Rate

12.02% of Baltimore's plays met the project's explosive-play definition, versus a 9.72% league average. Baltimore was especially dangerous on the ground, where 15.11% of runs gained at least 10 yards. San Francisco ranked second overall at 11.15% and Buffalo third at 10.90%.

Finding 8

NFL Offenses Were Most Efficient in the Second Quarter

Second-quarter plays generated 0.043 EPA/play, compared with -0.007 in the first, -0.002 in the third, and -0.023 in the fourth. The second quarter also had the highest success rate at 44.81%, while the fourth had the lowest success rate and highest turnover rate.

Conclusion

There is no single factor that determines NFL offensive success, but several characteristics repeatedly appear among the strongest offenses. Buffalo, Kansas City, Green Bay, and San Francisco ranked highest in EPA per play and also occupied the top four spots in third-down conversion rate. Many efficient offenses also generated explosive plays at above-average rates. Strategy changes substantially with game situation: teams pass much more when trailing, run more when protecting leads, and experience different efficiency levels depending on field position and quarter. Overall, successful offenses combine efficient play production, strong third-down performance, explosive-play ability, and situational flexibility.
